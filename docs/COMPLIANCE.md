# Data handling & compliance

This workflow processes **product-usage data belonging to identifiable users**
and uses it to **trigger outreach**. That places it squarely inside privacy and
anti-spam law. This document is written to be honest about obligations, not to
reassure you. **It is not legal advice** — consult your own counsel and DPO.

## What data is processed, and where it stays

| Data | Where it comes from | Where it lives | Leaves your control? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product events (`distinct_id`, event, timestamp, properties) | Your PostHog | Your PostHog + transiently in n8n | No — self-hosted / your cloud tenant |
| Person fields (email, name, company, plan, consent flag) | Your PostHog payload | Transiently in n8n; written to your Twenty on a real send | No — your instances |
| The **drafted message** | Generated by **your** LiteLLM proxy | Your LiteLLM; then Twenty/Slack | **Depends** — see LLM note below |
| Send/suppression state | n8n workflow static data (or your DB) | Your n8n | No |

**LLM note — the one external hop.** LiteLLM composes the message by calling an
**upstream LLM provider on your key.** The prompt contains the user's first
name, company, plan, and the usage signal. If your upstream is a hosted API
(Anthropic/OpenAI/etc.), **that context leaves your infrastructure** and is
subject to that provider's data-processing terms. To keep everything in-house,
point LiteLLM at a **local model** (Ollama/vLLM). Either way, this is a
processor relationship you must paper (DPA) and disclose.

## Consent & lawful basis (GDPR / CCPA)

- **You need a lawful basis** to process usage data for marketing outreach.
  Under **GDPR**, that is typically **legitimate interest** (with a balancing
  test and an easy opt-out) or **consent**. Legitimate interest for messaging
  *your own signed-up users* about *their own usage* is more defensible than for
  cold contact — but it is **not automatic**, and it must be documented.
- The `audience.yml` `require.marketing_consent` flag lets you **hard-require an
  explicit consent flag** before anyone is messaged. Turn it on if consent is
  your basis. Off by default so you make the choice deliberately.
- **CCPA/CPRA** (California) gives users the right to opt out of "sharing" and to
  deletion. Honor those signals in your suppression list.
- **Purpose limitation & data minimization:** only pass the fields the message
  actually needs. The templates use first name + company + the signal — not raw
  event dumps.
- **Transparency:** your privacy notice should say that product usage may be used
  to tailor communications, and how to opt out.

## Anti-spam obligations for the outreach itself

- **CAN-SPAM (US):** commercial email must have accurate headers, a valid
  physical postal address, a clear unsubscribe mechanism, and honor opt-outs
  promptly (10 business days). Applies to the email a rep sends off the back of
  this trigger.
- **CASL (Canada):** stricter — generally requires **consent** (express or
  implied) *before* sending a commercial electronic message, plus identification
  and unsubscribe. Implied consent from an existing business relationship is
  time-limited.
- **GDPR/ePrivacy (EU/UK):** electronic marketing generally needs consent, with a
  narrow "soft opt-in" for existing customers about similar products — check per
  jurisdiction.
- Because this workflow is **human-in-the-loop** (it drafts and notifies a rep;
  it does **not** auto-send to the end user), the actual send — and thus the
  anti-spam obligation — sits with the human who sends it. That is a feature:
  it keeps a person accountable for suppression, tone, and consent at the moment
  of contact. **It does not remove the obligation.**

## Your own users vs. cold contact — the distinction that matters

This workflow is built for messaging **people who already use your product** off
**their own** behavior. That is materially different from cold outreach to
purchased "intent" lists:

- The data is **first-party** and **volunteered through use**, not scraped or
  bought.
- There is usually a **pre-existing relationship** (signup/ToS), which supports
  legitimate-interest / soft-opt-in reasoning.
- The message references **their** activity, which they can verify.

None of that is a blank cheque. If you re-point this at people who are **not**
your users, or you message about things unrelated to their usage, you are back
in cold-contact territory with the stricter consent bar — and the
"surveillance" risk (see the operator checklist) rises sharply.

## Operator obligations baked into the config

- **Suppression list** (`audience.suppression_list`) — unsubscribes must land
  here and are honored before any send.
- **Internal/test exclusion** (`audience.exclude.email_domains`) — keep employees
  and QA out of outreach and out of your metrics.
- **Frequency caps & cooldowns** (per trigger) — prevent over-messaging, which is
  both a deliverability and a compliance problem.
- **Consent gate** (`require.marketing_consent`) — enable if consent is your basis.
- **DRY_RUN** — verify who would be contacted *before* contacting anyone.

## What this repo does NOT guarantee

- It does **not** make you compliant. Configuration and your legal basis do.
- It does **not** manage consent capture, DSAR handling, or data retention —
  those live in your product and your PostHog/Twenty instances.
- It does **not** verify email deliverability or manage bounce/complaint
  handling — that is on the sending channel your rep uses.
